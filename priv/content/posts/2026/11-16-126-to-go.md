*Update, November 26: As of today and Giggly 0.2, 129 of the 141 notebooks have nothing left waiting, and Giggly draws 568 of the 644 examples. Of the 76 it doesn't draw, 41 are ones we've decided not to. The other 35 are waiting on maps (32) and plotmath (3). What follows is the November 13 count, as it stood.*

In [A notebook for every page](/posts/a-notebook-for-every-page) I mentioned that every example Giggly can't draw yet says so in the same words, "Not in Giggly yet", so the notebooks double as a to-do list. A to-do list is also a scoreboard, so let's check the score.

There are 644 examples across the 141 notebooks. As of November 13, Giggly draws 493 of them. A month ago it drew 26.

## A month of drawing

All the notebooks were written in one go on October 15, with ggplot2's code for every example and Giggly's for the handful it could already draw. Everything since then is Giggly catching up:

![Fig. 1 — Examples Giggly draws, at the end of each day with a change to the notebooks.](/charts/2026-11-16-126-to-go/drawn.svg)

The best day was November 4, with 54 examples. That's not one big feature, it's a lot of small ones landing at once, because examples tend to wait on several things at a time. Look at the end of the line, though. From November 8 to 13 it only went up by 20. That's the long tail: the easy wins are gone, and most of what's left needs something big or something weird.

## Counting before building

Here's the part I like. Since every waiting example names what it's waiting for, picking the next thing to build isn't a matter of taste. You count.

Early on, on October 16, color was the obvious winner: 283 of the 619 waiting examples needed color or fill. But only six of them needed nothing else. Most were also waiting on jitter, a smooth, `labs()` or a scale function. So the count has to answer two questions: how many examples name a feature, and how many it actually finishes.

Every increment after that started the same way, with the count written into Giggly's design notes:

- **Text and titles** came next. Titles were named by 35 examples, second only to expressions in a mapping.
- **Scale functions** were Giggly's thinnest part at the time: 125 examples named one, and 30 needed nothing else.
- **Expressions in a mapping**, like `factor(cyl)` or `after_stat(count)`, were the most named thing of all, in 119 examples.
- **Size, shape and line type** were named by 86 examples, more than any other candidate.
- **The theme**: 25 examples needed nothing else.

Claude does the counting, which it is very good at, and then proposes the next increment, and I agree or don't. It's the most data-driven roadmap I've ever had, which is fitting for a charting library. It's also hard to argue with yourself when the numbers are right there.

## What's left

Of the 151 examples Giggly doesn't draw, 25 aren't waiting for anything. Most are about R itself rather than charts: saving a plot to a PDF, printing plots in a loop, putting the global theme back the way it was. Giggly draws SVG only and has no global settings, so those notebooks explain how Giggly does it instead and move on. The rest use `annotation_logticks()`, which ggplot2 itself has replaced, or would need thousands of R's random numbers copied into the notebook.

That leaves 126 waiting. Sorting each one by the biggest thing its note asks for:

![Fig. 2 — What the 126 waiting examples wait on. Each example is counted once, under the biggest thing it needs.](/charts/2026-11-16-126-to-go/left.svg)

- **Maps** (32). In R these lean on the `maps` and `sf` packages, which bring map data, geometries and coordinate reference systems. That's a whole geospatial stack, and a project of its own.
- **R's own machinery** (26). `ggproto()`, R's object system for extending ggplot2. `grid` grobs. Plotmath, R's little language for writing formulas in labels. `qplot()`. `%+%`. These are ggplot2 being an R library, and a lot of them don't have an obvious Elixir equivalent, or shouldn't. Some will get a Giggly answer of their own, and some never will.
- **Stat plumbing** (20). Choosing a layer's stat by name (`stat = "count"`), `stat_manual()`, `after_scale()` and `stage()`. Doable, just fiddly.
- **Statistical models** (11). Lines from `lm()`, a logistic regression from `glm()`, and quantile regression from the `quantreg` package. Each is a model fit that has to match R's numbers, and we know how that goes.
- **The `movies` dataset** (10). Nearly 60,000 films from IMDB, which ggplot2 keeps in a separate package because it's so big. The examples themselves are easy. It's just a lot of data to ship.
- **Theme palettes** (9). A theme setting the default color palettes, and `element_geom()`, which lets a theme set the colors layers draw with. Both are new in ggplot2 4.0.
- **Functions in a mapping** (8). Things like `max(count)` that need a whole column at once, where Giggly's expressions work row by row, plus a few places that take a function where Giggly takes a value.
- **Everything else** (10). An option here, a dataset there.

So what's next? Probably whatever the count says. But I'll admit the bottom of that chart is more tempting than the top. Ten examples for one dataset is a pretty good deal.
